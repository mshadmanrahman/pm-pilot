# Ten PM validation: is the trust problem real?

## What this is testing

One claim, and only one: **PMs get burned by AI-generated evidence they cannot check.**

If that is true, claim-level verification becomes PM Pilot's headline and the admin
framing becomes plumbing. If it is false, the headline stays where it is and the
verification feature stays a feature.

Do not test anything else in these conversations. No feature reactions, no pricing,
no reaction to the name. Those questions contaminate the answer and there is only
one chance to ask a person for the first time.

## The rule that governs every question

Ask about what they did, not what they think or would do. This is the Mom Test, and
`/pm-discovery:product-discovery interview` already enforces it. Applying it here:

| Do not ask | Ask instead |
|---|---|
| Would you find quote verification useful? | Walk me through the last time you used AI on customer feedback. |
| Do you worry about hallucinated quotes? | Did you check any of it against the source? How? |
| Would you pay for this? | What did you do with the output? Where did it end up? |

A "yes that would be useful" is worth nothing. A story about a specific Tuesday is
worth everything.

## The outreach message

Send as a DM. No link, no product name, no mention of PM Pilot.

> Hey [name], quick one and no pitch attached.
>
> I'm trying to learn something specific about how PMs actually work with AI on
> research. Last time you used it to summarise interviews, calls or customer
> feedback, did you check what it gave you against the source?
>
> Curious either way, including if the answer is no. Two lines is a fine reply, or
> 15 minutes if you would rather talk.

Why it is shaped this way: it asks about one past action, it makes "no" an
acceptable answer out loud, and it costs them nothing to reply. The moment it
mentions a tool, every answer bends toward being polite about the tool.

## The five questions

For anyone who replies or takes the call. Follow the story, not the list.

1. Walk me through the last time. What were you summarising, and what did you use?
2. What did you do with the output? Who saw it?
3. Did you check any of it against the source? If yes, how, and how long did it take?
4. Was there a moment that made you start checking, or stop checking?
5. Has anything an AI produced for you reached someone else before you spotted a
   problem in it?

Question 5 is the one that matters. Ask it last, so it does not colour the rest.

## How to read the answers

Count these separately. Do not average them into a verdict.

- **Burned, and knows it.** They can name an incident. This is the signal. Even two
  of ten is a real finding, because the incident is specific and quotable.
- **Checks by hand.** No incident, but they built a manual habit. They already pay
  the cost in time, so the problem is real and the pain is dull rather than sharp.
- **Does not check, not worried.** The honest negative. Count it.
- **Does not check, and goes quiet when asked.** Also a negative. Do not read
  discomfort as agreement.
- **Does not use AI on evidence at all.** Out of scope. Does not count toward ten.

## The decision this feeds

Ten conversations, two weeks, then pick one:

- **Four or more with a nameable incident**: the trust framing leads. Rewrite the
  headline, promote verification out of `pm-discovery`, and use their words, not
  the Torres number, as the proof.
- **Mostly manual checkers, no incidents**: the pain is time, not trust. Keep the
  current headline and sell verification as the thing that removes a chore.
- **Mostly "not worried"**: the thesis is wrong. Jahin and I were two PMs of the
  same kind reasoning from the same experience. Keep the admin framing and stop
  building on the trust story.

Write the count into this file when it is done, including the negatives.

## Who to ask

Ten people, and not ten people who already like the project. Mix:

- PMs from the ADPList mentee pool who do their own research
- The Ostad cohort, but only those working as PMs now
- LinkedIn network PMs who have never seen PM Pilot
- At least two who have told you in the past that they do not use AI much

The last group is the one that keeps this honest. A sample drawn only from people
who follow your work will tell you the trust problem is real whether or not it is.
